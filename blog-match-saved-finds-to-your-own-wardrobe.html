Wardrobe planning

Before another find: give your Hipobuy spreadsheet an outfit test

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read

A jacket can look right in a photo and still need an entire outfit to go with it. Use a small outfit map to see what works with clothes you already own.

The jacket looks great. What would you wear with it?

A saved jacket can look perfect in its own photo and still be awkward with everything in your wardrobe. Maybe the trousers you pictured are another unsaved purchase. Maybe the shoes only work with a different pair of trousers. Suddenly one interesting find has turned into three things to buy.

Before adding more links to a Hipobuy spreadsheet, try giving each clothing idea a small outfit test. You do not need a mood board or a whole new wardrobe. You need to name the pieces you would actually wear together.

Start with clothes you already reach for

Pick a normal occasion: a walk to get coffee, a day on campus, or an evening meal. Write down a bottom, a pair of shoes and a layer you already own and enjoy wearing. Be specific enough to picture them: “straight dark jeans” says more than “pants.”

Then place the saved item into that outfit. What does it replace? If the answer is “nothing; I also need new trousers and shoes,” keep that dependency visible. It might still be an outfit you want, but it is a bigger decision than buying one jacket.

Color is one part of this. Shape matters too: a longer top can cover a jacket hem you wanted to show, while a bulky layer may change the silhouette in the reference photo. Put the question in the sheet and, where possible, try the combination with a similar piece you already own. An outfit idea cannot tell you whether a particular garment will fit.

The point is not to award a score to every item. “On hold until I can name the shoes” is a perfectly useful result. So is “I already own something that does the same job.”

Download the blank outfit-map CSV and use it as a second tab beside your product research. It has room for an existing top or layer, bottoms, shoes, the occasion and any extra purchase the outfit depends on. The file is blank so your own wardrobe supplies the examples.

Browse for the missing role, then stop

A small gap makes browsing easier. “A quieter top to wear with patterned trousers” is a clearer brief than “something nice.” The Fashionreps catalog currently separates clothing into categories including jackets, hoodies, T-shirts and pants. Those category views can help you look for a particular role without starting another open-ended list.

Save the reference that answers your brief, then return to the outfit map. Does it use clothes you already own? Does it duplicate a saved item? Would you still want it without the rest of the photographed look? Those questions are easier to answer while the outfit is still fresh in your mind.

A catalog photo is a starting point for styling, not proof of fabric feel, fit or how the color will look beside your own clothes. Keep the normal source and measurement checks in the product tab; the outfit tab answers a different question: whether this item has a place to go.

Leave room for one favorite

You do not have to turn every purchase into a sensible basic. A bold piece can be the reason an outfit feels like yours. Give it one believable combination rather than inventing three weak ones to justify keeping it.

Before closing the sheet, write a single sentence: “I would wear this with ___ to ___.” If the blanks are easy to fill, you have a clearer reason to keep researching. If they are not, leaving the row on hold costs nothing.
